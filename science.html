Physics

Physics is the study of matter, energy, and the laws that govern the universe. It seeks to explain how objects move, how forces interact, and how fields shape the behavior of particles.

Main branches include:

  • Classical mechanics – motion, forces, and energy at everyday scales.
  • Thermodynamics – heat, work, energy transfer, and entropy.
  • Electromagnetism – electric and magnetic fields and their interactions.
  • Quantum physics – behavior of particles at atomic and subatomic scales.

Key concepts:

  • Newton's Laws describe motion, inertia, force, and action/reaction.
  • Conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed.
  • Wave-particle duality explains how light and matter can behave as both particles and waves.
//--Chemistry section--

Chemistry

Chemistry is the study of substances, their properties, and how they change through reactions. It explores the composition of matter and the principles that govern chemical behavior.

Main branches include:

  • Organic chemistry – carbon-based compounds and life-related molecules.
  • Inorganic chemistry – minerals, metals, and non-carbon compounds.
  • Physical chemistry – the physics of chemical systems, including energy and reaction rates.
  • Analytical chemistry – techniques for identifying and measuring substances.

Key concepts:

  • The periodic table organizes elements by atomic number and recurring chemical properties.
  • Chemical bonding explains how atoms connect through ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds.
  • States of matter describe how solids, liquids, gases, and plasmas differ in structure and energy.
//--Biology section--

Biology

Biology is the study of living organisms and their interactions with each other and the environment. It covers life from the smallest cells to entire ecosystems.

Main branches include:

  • Cell biology – structure and function of cells, the basic units of life.
  • Genetics – heredity, DNA, and how traits are transmitted.
  • Ecology – relationships between organisms and their environments.
  • Evolution – the change of species over time by natural selection.

Key concepts:

  • Cell theory states that all living things are made of cells and that cells arise from existing cells.
  • DNA carries genetic instructions used in growth, development, and reproduction.
  • Natural selection explains how traits that improve survival and reproduction become more common.
